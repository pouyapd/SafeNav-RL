TITLE
Deadlocks of Learned Navigation Policies Behind a Control-Barrier-Function Safety Filter: Mechanisms and Planning-Based Recovery

ABSTRACT (250 words)
A control-barrier-function (CBF) safety filter can keep a learned navigation policy collision-free, but the robot may then stall before reaching its goal. We study such deadlocks for a simulated unicycle whose speed is limited by a one-step discrete-time CBF filter. Two filter properties frame the analysis: its one-step guarantee does not depend on the turn rate, and near an obstacle the headings that permit motion shrink to a half-plane. Classifying the timeouts of ten proximal policy optimisation agents, in scenarios that a geometric oracle confirmed solvable, reveals two mechanisms whose balance depends on training. Agents trained without the filter are held at the boundary by the filter, whereas agents trained with it mostly stop on their own before it; both effects replicated on unused scenarios. Three pre-registered training interventions (a published correction penalty, a Beta policy, a reward without a risk term) removed most self-stops, but filter-blocked stalls replaced them, and total deadlock changed by at most about 5 points. A recovery layer that leaves the filter unchanged and plans multi-step manoeuvres at stalls with a shortest-path cost reduced total deadlock from 53–59 % to below 1 % and raised success by 39–47 points in a pre-registered test, without collisions; about 14 % of episodes still timed out while moving. The result held on new scenario sets, with position noise and with a sensor-built map, but a wrong map restored about 20 % deadlock, and the planner alone, given the exact map, was more successful (95 % against 85–86 %). All results are from simulation.

KEYWORDS (6)
safe reinforcement learning; control barrier functions; safety filter; deadlock; recovery planning; mobile robot navigation
